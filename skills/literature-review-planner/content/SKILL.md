# Literature Review Planner

A systematic approach to conducting comprehensive literature reviews with AI assistance, from initial discovery through final synthesis.

---

## Overview

This skill transforms the literature review process from overwhelming to manageable. Rather than drowning in hundreds of papers, you will work through a strategic, prioritized reading plan that helps you focus on what matters most for your research question.

**When to Use This Skill:**
- Starting a new literature review or systematic review
- Need to find relevant research papers on a specific topic
- Want to organize and synthesize findings from multiple sources
- Looking to identify gaps or contradictions in existing research
- Building a research roadmap or reading guide

---

## Step 1: Define Your Research Context

Before beginning your literature review, establish clear boundaries for your research question. A well-defined scope prevents overwhelm and ensures actionable results.

### Elements of a Strong Research Question

**Primary Topic:** What is your main subject area?
- Example: "gut-brain axis and mood disorders"

**Specific Focus:** What particular aspects interest you?
- Example: "probiotic interventions in clinical trials"

**Time Boundaries:** What period should papers cover?
- Example: "last 5 years" for staying current with rapidly evolving fields

**Study Types:** What kinds of evidence do you need?
- Example: "systematic reviews, RCTs, and mechanistic studies"

### Structured Research Question Template

When presenting your research to the AI assistant, include:

```
I'm beginning a literature review on [TOPIC]. I need to understand:
- [Specific mechanism or concept 1]
- [Evidence type or intervention 2]
- [Additional dimension 3]

Search [DATABASE] for the most relevant papers from [TIMEFRAME] and create a structured reading guide that helps me prioritize what to read first.

Organize everything into a [OUTPUT FORMAT] that serves as my research roadmap with [DESIGN ELEMENTS].
```

## Step 2: Provide Essential Context

### Required Context

**Database Access:** Connect to databases like PubMed to enable comprehensive searches, not just papers you already have. This expands your research beyond your existing collection.

### Optional Enhancements

**Extended Thinking Mode:** Enable this feature when you need Claude to spend additional time:
- Evaluating research quality and relevance
- Identifying subtle patterns across papers
- Creating more nuanced organizational structures
- Providing deeper analytical insights

**Citation Management Tools:** Consider connecting reference managers to:
- Track papers you've already read
- Organize new findings systematically
- Export directly to your writing workflow

## Step 3: Leverage Follow-up Prompts

After your initial research roadmap, continue the conversation to deepen your analysis.

### Validate Emerging Patterns

As you read, you may notice patterns across papers. Test these observations before building arguments around them.

**Example Prompt:**
> "I want to say most recent studies focus on X while older ones focused on Y. Does this hold across these papers, or are there contradictions? Help me understand why or why not, and flag any papers that contradict this pattern."

### Investigate Contradictions

When sources conflict, dig deeper to understand why and find resolution.

**Example Prompt:**
> "Paper A claims [finding] while Paper B claims the opposite. Analyze why they contradict and search for other papers that might resolve or explain this contradiction."

### Fill Coverage Gaps

Identify and address gaps in your literature coverage as you progress.

**Example Prompt:**
> "I'm seeing a gap in my coverage of [specific mechanism/population/intervention]. Search for papers specifically addressing this, evaluate them, and add the most relevant ones to my roadmap. Where do they fit in my reading sequence?"

## Step 4: Interpret Output Effectively

### Understanding Research Roadmaps

A well-constructed research roadmap typically includes:

**Phase 1: Foundation Papers**
- High-impact systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- Papers that synthesize hundreds of studies
- Theoretical framework establishment
- Recommended timing: Week 1

**Phase 2: Mechanism and Evidence**
- Mechanistic studies explaining how relationships work
- Clinical trials showing intervention effects
- Primary research with novel findings
- Recommended timing: Weeks 2-3

**Phase 3: Gap Analysis**
- Emerging research and cutting-edge findings
- Contradictory results requiring critical analysis
- Papers challenging mainstream conclusions
- Recommended timing: Week 4+

### Document Components

Your output may include:
- Visual landscape overview showing paper distribution
- Detailed profiles for each priority paper
- Relevance ratings and key findings summaries
- Color-coded categories for study type
- Space for your annotations and notes

## Best Practices

### Critical Verification

Always verify critical citations before including them in your work. AI assistants excel at tracking evidence, but double-check:
- Direct quotes against original papers
- Statistical claims against reported data
- Author attributions for specific findings

Use AI assistance to locate relevant passages quickly, but verify exact wording yourself.

### Working with Full Documents

AI-generated previews show basic structure, but complete documents often contain:
- Full formatting and color-coding
- Design elements enhancing readability
- Complete annotation spaces
- Visual hierarchies not visible in previews

Always open the actual file to see the true form and make edits.

### Expanding Your Infrastructure

Consider connecting additional tools to enhance your workflow:
- Google Drive integration for searching existing collections
- Reference managers to identify already-read papers
- Additional database connectors beyond PubMed
- Cloud storage for organizing new findings

### Design Language Matters

Include specific design cues in your prompts:
- "beautifully designed" for enhanced formatting
- "professional formatting" for academic standards
- "premium research tool" for polished outputs
- Color-coding preferences for organization
- Visual hierarchy requirements

Without explicit design instructions, outputs default to basic styling.

## Troubleshooting

### Common Issues

**Overwhelmed by Results:** Narrow your search with additional filters or specific sub-questions. Break broad topics into focused searches.

**Too Few Results:** Expand time boundaries, consider broader synonyms, or include related terminology in your search.

**Irrelevant Papers:** Refine your research question to be more specific about population, intervention, or outcome.

**Unclear Organization:** Request reorganization with different criteria (chronological, methodological, thematic).

### Quality Indicators

High-quality literature reviews demonstrate:
- Systematic coverage of relevant sources
- Clear organizational logic
- Critical evaluation of evidence quality
- Identification of patterns and contradictions
- Recognition of gaps in current knowledge
- Synthesis leading to new insights

Choose the approach that matches your research goals and time constraints.
