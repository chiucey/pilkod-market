# Depth Dialogue Counseling Skill - Reference Documentation

## Theoretical Foundation Integration

### 1. Existentialist Psychology

**Key Figures**: Viktor Frankl, Rollo May, Irvin Yalom

**Core Concepts**:
- **Existential Anxiety**: Anxiety arising from facing freedom, death, loneliness, and meaninglessness
- **Will to Meaning**: The fundamental human motivation to find meaning in life
- **Bearing Responsibility**: We are free, but must take responsibility for our choices
- **Existential Choice**: Making authentic choices within limited possibilities

**Counseling Application**:
- Help users face "existential dilemmas" (choice, responsibility, death)
- Assist in finding personal meaning
- Support users in having courage to bear the weight of choice

### 2. Humanistic Psychology

**Key Figures**: Carl Rogers, Abraham Maslow

**Core Concepts**:
- **Self-Actualization Tendency**: The inherent human drive toward positive development
- **Unconditional Positive Regard**: Being fully accepted is the foundation of human growth
- **Client-Centered Approach**: Trust that the client is the expert on their own problems
- **Fully Functioning Person**: One who fully functions exhibits open experience, autonomous choice, and intimacy with existence

**Counseling Application**:
- Provide a safe, accepting dialogue space
- Use reflective listening
- Trust in users' capacity for self-growth

### 3. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

**Key Figures**: Aaron Beck, Albert Ellis

**Core Concepts**:
- **Cognitive Triangle**: Situation → Thoughts → Emotions/Behaviors
- **Cognitive Distortions**: Common patterns of irrational thinking
- **Core Beliefs**: Deep-seated beliefs about self, world, and future
- **Intermediate Beliefs**: Rules/assumptions formed to serve core beliefs

**Counseling Application**:
- Help identify thinking patterns
- Test the validity of assumptions
- Explore the relationship between thoughts and emotions

### 4. Mindfulness and Eastern Philosophy

**Core Concepts**:
- **Awareness**: Clear recognition of present moment physical and mental states
- **Acceptance**: Allowing emotions and thoughts to exist without resistance
- **Holistic View**: Mind and body as an integrated whole
- **Yin-Yang Balance**: Integration and balance of opposites

**Counseling Application**:
- Invite users to be with their emotions
- Use body scan to become aware of emotions
- Integrative rather than confrontational thinking

## Questioning Techniques in Detail

### Socratic Questioning

**Characteristics**:
- Open-ended: Encourages deep exploration
- Exploratory: Does not preset answers
- Reflective: Promotes self-awareness
- Gradual: Progressively deeper

**Types**:

1. **Clarification Questions**
   - "Can you explain...?"
   - "What I'm hearing is... is that correct?"

2. **Specificity Questions**
   - "Can you give a specific example?"
   - "What exactly happened then?"

3. **Timeline Questions**
   - "When did this start?"
   - "Has anything changed recently?"

4. **Impact Questions**
   - "How has this affected your life?"
   - "What do others think about this issue?"

5. **Values Questions**
   - "What is truly important to you?"
   - "If this problem were solved, how would you feel?"

6. **Possibility Questions**
   - "Have you considered other possible explanations?"
   - "If there were no constraints, how would you choose?"

7. **Reflective Questions**
   - "What does this remind you of?"
   - "Have you noticed any patterns?"

### Paradox Questioning Technique

**Definition**: By posing seemingly contradictory questions, help users view their situation from a new angle

**Examples**:
- "Are you willing to continue like this?"
- "What you're afraid of — if it actually happened, what would happen?"
- "If you continue to not choose, what would happen?"

### Metaphor and Imagery Techniques

**Definition**: Use symbolic language to help users access deeper levels of experience

**Examples**:
- "If you could describe your current state with a color, what would it be?"
- "If your emotion were an animal, what would it be?"
- "What does your inner child want to tell you?"

## Common Cognitive Distortion Patterns

1. **All-or-Nothing Thinking**: Viewing things in black-and-white terms
2. **Catastrophizing**: Overestimating the likelihood and impact of negative events
3. **Mind Reading**: Assuming to know what others are thinking
4. **Emotional Reasoning**: Inferring reality from emotional states
5. **Labeling**: Attaching fixed labels to self or others
6. **Personalization**: Taking responsibility for things outside your control
7. **Mental Filtering**: Focusing only on negatives while ignoring positives
8. **Disqualifying the Positive**: Rejecting positive experiences
9. **Overgeneralization**: Drawing broad conclusions from single events
10. **Minimizing**: Dismissing positive experiences as unimportant

## Crisis Recognition Signals

### Suicide Risk Signals
- Directly expressing not wanting to live
- Talking about suicide methods
- Giving away prized possessions
- Withdrawing from loved ones
- Sudden calm mood (may be calm after decision to act)

### Situations Requiring Referral
- Self-harm/suicidal ideation → Professional crisis intervention
- Severe trauma response → Trauma treatment specialist support
- Suspected mental disorder → Psychiatric evaluation
- Substance/alcohol dependence → Addiction treatment

### Referral Suggestions
"I care about what you're going through, and these feelings seem very heavy for you. I think you might need more professional support to help you through this time. Have you considered reaching out to a counselor or crisis hotline?"
