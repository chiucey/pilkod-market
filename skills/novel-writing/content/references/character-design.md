# Character Design Guide

## Protagonist Design Elements

### Basic Information
- **Name**: Origin meaning, historical context
- **Age**: Physical age, psychological age
- **Appearance**: Height, build, facial features, distinctive traits
- **Social Identity**: Profession, class, status

### Psychological Layer
- **Core Desire**: Deepest inner longing
- **Core Fear**: What they most dread facing
- **Personality Traits**: MBTI/Big Five reference
- **Behavioral Motivation**: Deep reasons for actions
- **Values**: Unshakeable beliefs

### Character Arc
- **Starting State**: Psychological state at story's beginning
- **Change Catalyst**: Events triggering transformation
- **Growth Journey**: Inner changes process
- **Ending State**: State at story's conclusion

### Character Relationship Network
- **Family**: Parents, siblings, children
- **Lover**: Partner, romantic interest
- **Friends**: Close friends, acquaintances
- **Enemies**: Main opponents, rivals
- **Mentor**: One who guides growth
- **Followers**: Those who trust the protagonist

## Dialogue Design Principles

### Everyone Has a Unique Voice
- **Word Choice**: Education level, cultural background
- **Sentence Length**: Impatient or calm personality
- **Speaking Habits**: Catchphrases, fillers
- **Avoidance Patterns**: What they don't directly say

### Dialogue Functions
- **Advance Plot**: Reveal important information
- **Show Character**: See personality through language
- **Build Relationships**: Emotional changes in interaction
- **Create Conflict**: Disagreements, arguments

### Dialogue Examples Comparison

**Young and Direct Type:**
> "Are you crazy? This is completely impossible!"
> "I don't care what you think, I'm doing this anyway."
> "Don't lecture me, the facts are what they are!"

**Calm and Reserved Type:**
> "Perhaps we could approach this from a different angle."
> "I understand your concerns, but I'm afraid that's not enough."
> "Some things require time to prove."

**Humorous and Teasing Type:**
> "Oh, wonderful, another 'surprise'."
> "I knew things wouldn't be this simple."
> "Looks like the fortune gods are in a good mood today."

## Character Growth Design

### Positive Growth Arc
- **Start**: Has flaws or faces difficulties
- **Development**: Learns and grows through challenges
- **Turning Point**: Major event catalyzes change
- **End**: Overcomes flaws, achieves growth

### Negative Corruption Arc
- **Start**: Positive or idealistic
- **Development**: Faces temptation or difficulties
- **Turning Point**: Makes wrong choice
- **End**: Falls or is destroyed

### Flat Character Arc
- **Feature**: Steadfast beliefs unchanged
- **Purpose**: Serve as reference for other characters
- **Value**: Show the power of certain qualities

## Character Relationship Dynamics

### Relationship Development Ladder
1. **Strangers**: Unacquainted
2. **Acquainted**: Aware of each other's existence
3. **Contact**: Begin interacting
4. **Familiar**: Gradually getting to know
5. **Trust**: Can rely on each other
6. **Intimacy**: Can talk about anything
7. **Conflict**: Tension arises
8. **Test**: Relationship tested
9. **Repair/Break**: Relationship changes
10. **Deepen/End**: New state of relationship
