# Writing Style Guide

## Narrative Perspective Selection

### First Person (I)
**Advantages**:
- Genuine and direct emotion
- Strong reader immersion
- Natural and smooth narration
- Suitable for growth stories

**Disadvantages**:
- Limited perspective
- Difficult to show others' inner thoughts
- Single writing approach
- Not suitable for multi-threaded narratives

**Best Used For**:
- Personal growth stories
- Psychological exploration
- Emotional novels
- Mystery and detective fiction

### Third Person Limited
**Advantages**:
- Can delve into character's inner world
- Relatively flexible perspective
- Can control information volume
- Strong adaptability

**Disadvantages**:
- Transitions require skill
- May feel stiff
- Need clear rules

**Best Used For**:
- Mainstream novels
- Character-driven stories
- Complex plot works

### Third Person Omniscient
**Advantages**:
- Widest perspective
- Free to switch
- Can show everyone's inner thoughts
- Most information

**Disadvantages**:
- Weaker immersion
- May spoil surprises
- Requires advanced skills
- Can become preachy

**Best Used For**:
- Epic works
- Ensemble casts
- Social observation
- Historical fiction

## Narrative Pacing

### Problem: Too Fast
- Scenes rushing
- Lacking detail
- Emotion doesn't have time to settle
- Reader fatigue

**Solutions**:
- Add scene descriptions
- Include inner monologue
- Design emotional pauses
- Insert necessary background

### Problem: Too Slow
- Long-winded and dragging
- Repetitive information
- Stagnant plot
- Bored readers

**Solutions**:
- Delete redundant descriptions
- Speed up plot advancement
- Add conflict events
- Increase information density

## Description Techniques

### Scene Description
```
【Excellent Example】
The sunset's afterglow fell on the ancient city walls, turning the weathered bricks golden-red. From the distant bell tower came the long toll of bells, echoing through the valley. He stood at the city gate, looking at this road leading to the unknown, with both anticipation and trepidation in his heart.

【Analysis】
- Visual: Sunset, old bricks, bell tower
- Auditory: Bell sounds
- Psychological: Anticipation, trepidation
- Spatial sense: City gate, valley
- Atmosphere: Warm yet slightly melancholic
```

### Character Description
```
【Excellent Example】
She was around thirty, with long black hair casually tied back, a few stray strands falling across her face. Her eyes were a very light brown, like autumn leaves, yet at this moment they held an inexplicable gleam. The corners of her mouth slightly lifted, as if about to smile at any moment, yet also as if nothing was happening.

【Analysis】
- Physical features: Long hair, brown eyes
- Expression details: Corners of mouth lifted
- Hidden information: Sense of mystery
- Room for imagination: Unclear background
```

### Psychological Description
```
【Excellent Example】
He suddenly realized that he had been running away. Running away from that question, from that answer, from possible failure. But now, standing at this crossroads of choice, for the first time he had no escape.

【Analysis】
- Cognitive change: Realizing
- Reflective content: Running away
- Current situation: No escape
- Inner conflict: Fear vs. courage
```

### Dialogue Description
```
【Excellent Example】
"Are you really going to do this?" Her voice trembled slightly.
He turned around, looking into her eyes: "This is the only way."
"But..."
"No buts." He cut her off. "Some things must be done by someone."

【Analysis】
- Action paired with dialogue: Turning around, eye contact
- Tone markers: Voice trembling
- Interruption technique: Emphasizing determination
- Subtext: Hint of sacrifice
```

## Language Style

### Concise and Powerful
```
【Verbose】
He quickly ran to that place, where a person was waiting for him.

【Concise】
When he arrived, that person was already waiting.
```

### Specific and Vivid
```
【Abstract】
He felt very happy.

【Specific】
An involuntary smile crept across his face, his eyes curving into two crescent moons.
```

### Sensory Richness
```
【Single Sense】
He walked into the room and saw a painting.

【Multi-Sensory】
He pushed open the heavy wooden door, and a wave of stale wood scent greeted him. The room wasn't large, with a faded oil painting hanging on the wall depicting a golden wheat field, with sunlight breaking through the clouds just right.
```

### Rhythm Variation
```
【Monotonous Rhythm】
He walked to the door. He pushed open the door. He walked into the room. He saw her.

【Varied Rhythm】
He walked to that familiar door and stopped. Took a deep breath, then pushed it open.
Inside the room, she sat with her back to him, gazing out the window.
He walked over gently: "I'm back."
```

## Common Problems

### 1. Show vs. Tell
```
【Over-telling】
She was very sad, tears streaming down uncontrollably.

【Showing】
She sat by the window, saying nothing. Tears slipped silently, falling onto the back of her tightly clenched hand. She didn't wipe them away, just stared blankly out the window.

【Mixed Usage】
She was sad. (Telling)
But she didn't cry. (Showing)
Some pain cannot be cried away. (Telling)
```

### 2. Information Dumping
```
【Problem】
His name was Li Ming, he was thirty years old, he was a policeman, he had been a policeman for ten years. His father was also a policeman, this had always been the family tradition...

【Solution】
"Detective Li," a colleague handed him a file, "after ten years, this case finally has a clue."
He took the file, his gaze falling on the yellowed old photograph. His father's figure, he knew too well.

【Techniques】
- Integrate information into dialogue
- Naturally bring out background
- Show through actions
- Distribute information
```

### 3. Clichés
```
【Cliché】
Her heart was racing like a deer, he was like a white knight, and they lived happily ever after.

【Fresh】
She didn't know how to describe this feeling. It was like a stone dropped into a still lake, the ripples refusing to settle. And he, it seemed, was that stone.
```

### 4. Perspective Confusion
```
【Problem】
He walked into the room. She was reading. She looked up at the sound of footsteps.

【Fixed】
He walked into the room and saw her reading.
"Back?" She looked up, her gaze lifting from the pages to him.

【Techniques】
- Clarify current perspective character
- Connect with "saw" and similar words
- New line or paragraph for perspective shift
- Maintain perspective consistency
```

## Literary Devices

### Metaphor
- Image and concrete
- Avoid the old
- Serve the theme
- Fresh and unique

### Personification
- Endow with emotion
- Vivid and lively
- Environment rendering
- Atmosphere creation

### Parallelism
- Enhance momentum
- Emotional progression
- Strong rhythm
- Memorable impression

### Contrast
- Highlight differences
- Deepen theme
- Enhance tension
- Character development
