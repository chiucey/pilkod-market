# Worldbuilding Guide

## Worldbuilding Layer System

### Macro Layer: World Foundation

#### Natural World
- **Geography**: Continents, oceans, mountains, rivers
- **Climate**: Temperature, precipitation, seasonal changes
- **Natural Resources**: Minerals, plants, animals
- **Natural Disasters**: Earthquakes, floods, plagues

#### Social Structure
- **Political System**: Monarchy, democracy, theocracy
- **Economic System**: Agriculture, commerce, industry
- **Social Classes**: Nobility, commoners, slaves/class rigidity or mobility
- **Legal System**: Statutory law, customary law, trial system

#### Cultural System
- **Religious Beliefs**: Deities, rituals, religious organizations
- **Philosophy**: Outlook on life, values, moral views
- **Art Forms**: Painting, music, literature, architecture
- **Festivals**: Traditional celebrations, commemorative days

### Meso Layer: Social Operation

#### City Design
- **City Layout**: City walls, streets, district division
- **Core Buildings**: Palace, temples, markets
- **Residential Areas**: Living areas for different classes
- **Infrastructure**: Water supply, drainage, fire prevention

#### Power Distribution
- **Major Nations**: Political system, territory, strength
- **Local Powers**: Warlords, nobles, gangs
- **Religious Powers**: Churches, cultivators, temples
- **Commercial Powers**: Guilds, families, consortiums

#### Industry Ecosystem
- **Major Industries**: Agriculture, crafts, commerce
- **Emerging Industries**: Changes brought by technological innovation
- **Gray Industries**: Smuggling, black markets, espionage
- **Industry Rules**: Guilds, unions, regulations

### Micro Layer: Life Details

#### Daily Life
- **Eating Habits**: Food, drinks, dining customs
- **Clothing**: Fashion style, status markers
- **Living Environment**: House structure, furniture
- **Transportation**: Vehicles, traffic rules

#### Social Customs
- **Greeting Etiquette**: Greetings, honorifics
- **Weddings and Funerals**: Ceremonies, births
- **Festival Activities**: Celebration methods, taboos
- **Social Activities**: Gatherings, entertainment, competitions

## Worldbuilding Checklist

### Basic Settings
- [ ] What era does the story take place in?
- [ ] What are the basic rules of this world?
- [ ] What does the main geographical environment look like?
- [ ] What are the core features of the social system?

### Political Landscape
- [ ] What are the major forces/nations?
- [ ] What are their relationships?
- [ ] Is there war or conflict?
- [ ] What is the core of power?

### Cultural Features
- [ ] What are the mainstream values of this society?
- [ ] What unique cultural traditions exist?
- [ ] What role do religious beliefs play in life?
- [ ] What are art and entertainment like?

### Economic Development
- [ ] What is the economic foundation of this world?
- [ ] What is the wealth gap like?
- [ ] How are commercial activities conducted?
- [ ] What is the monetary system like?

### Daily Life
- [ ] How does an ordinary person spend their day?
- [ ] What do they eat, wear, live in?
- [ ] What entertainment activities are there?
- [ ] What is the family structure like?

## Worldbuilding Design Principles

### 1. Serve the Story
- World settings should serve plot needs
- Don't create settings for the sake of it
- Highlight details relevant to the story

### 2. Details Determine Authenticity
- Daily details make the world more credible
- Unique details create distinctiveness
- Avoid generic settings

### 3. Logical Consistency
- Settings must be mutually consistent
- Consider implications and consequences
- Avoid inexplicable contradictions

### 4. Art of Leaving Space
- Don't need to be comprehensive
- Give readers room for imagination
- Reveal more settings gradually

## Creation Sequence Suggestions

### Step 1: Core Settings
Determine the most core worldbuilding elements that directly affect plot and characters.

### Step 2: Plot Needs
Gradually add necessary worldbuilding details according to story requirements.

### Step 3: Detail Filling
After main settings are complete, add life details for authenticity.

### Step 4: Conflicts and Contradictions
Design contradictions and conflicts within the worldbuilding as story drivers.

## Worldbuilding Display Techniques

### Through Character Experience
- Protagonist's observations when first arriving at a new place
- Character's inner feelings and reactions
- Interactions with locals

### Through Conflict Presentation
- Reactions when rules are broken
- Collisions between different cultural backgrounds
- Discomfort of outsiders

### Through Detail Implication
- Values reflected in architectural style
- History behind daily customs
- Technology level reflected in implements and goods
