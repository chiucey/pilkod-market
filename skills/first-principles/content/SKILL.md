# First Principles Thinking

This skill provides a structured approach to solving difficult problems using Elon Musk's "First Principles Thinking" and his 5-step engineering algorithm. It helps you break out of conventional "reasoning by analogy" to innovate from fundamental truths.

## Core Philosophy

First principles thinking is the practice of actively questioning every assumption you think you know about a given problem, boiling it down to the most fundamental truths (things you are absolutely sure are true), and reasoning up from there.

*   **Reasoning by analogy:** Building knowledge and solving problems based on prior assumptions, beliefs, and widely held "best practices" (copying what others do with slight variations).
*   **Reasoning from first principles:** Thinking like a physicist. Deconstruct the problem to its atomic level and reconstruct a new, more effective solution.

## The 3-Step First Principles Framework

When faced with a complex problem, apply this 3-step framework:

1.  **Identify and define current assumptions:** Write down the existing beliefs, constraints, or conventional wisdom about the problem.
2.  **Break down the problem into its fundamental principles:** Ask powerful questions to uncover basic truths.
3.  **Create new solutions from scratch:** Recombine the fundamental elements into a new, optimized solution.

See [references/examples.md](references/examples.md) for real-world examples of how this was applied at SpaceX and Tesla.

## Elon Musk's 5-Step Algorithm

For process optimization, product design, and manufacturing, strictly follow this 5-step algorithm in exact order. **Do not skip steps or change the sequence.**

1.  **Question every requirement:** Each requirement must have the name of the real person who made it (not a department). Question it, no matter how smart the person is. Make the requirements less dumb.
2.  **Delete any part or process you can:** If you do not end up adding back at least 10% of what you deleted, you didn't delete enough.
3.  **Simplify and optimize:** Only do this *after* step two. A common mistake is to simplify a part or process that should not exist at all.
4.  **Accelerate cycle time:** Every process can be sped up. But only do this *after* following the first three steps.
5.  **Automate:** That comes last. Do not automate until requirements are questioned, unnecessary parts deleted, and the process is simplified and accelerated.

## Application Guide

When a user asks you to apply first principles thinking or optimize a process:

1.  Start by explicitly listing the current assumptions or the existing process steps.
2.  Apply the 3-Step Framework or the 5-Step Algorithm systematically.
3.  Present your analysis clearly, contrasting the "Analogy approach" with the "First Principles approach."
