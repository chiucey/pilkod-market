# First Principles Thinking Examples

This document provides real-world examples of how Elon Musk applied First Principles Thinking to disrupt industries. Use these examples to guide your own analysis and solution generation.

## Example 1: SpaceX Rocket Costs

**The Problem:** In 2002, Elon Musk wanted to send a rocket to Mars. He discovered that purchasing a rocket cost up to $65 million, an astronomical price that made his vision impossible.

**Reasoning by Analogy:** "Rockets are expensive because they have always been expensive. The aerospace industry has established the cost, and we must accept it or find a slightly cheaper supplier."

**Reasoning from First Principles:**
1.  **Identify assumptions:** "Rockets cost $65 million to build."
2.  **Break down to fundamental principles:** "What is a rocket made of? Aerospace-grade aluminum alloys, plus some titanium, copper, and carbon fiber. What is the value of those materials on the commodity market?" It turned out the materials cost was only about 2% of the typical rocket price.
3.  **Create new solutions:** Instead of buying a finished rocket, Musk created his own company, purchased the raw materials cheaply, and built the rockets himself. SpaceX cut the price of launching a rocket by nearly 10x.

## Example 2: Tesla Battery Costs

**The Problem:** Early in Tesla's history, people said battery packs were too expensive to make cheap electric cars.

**Reasoning by Analogy:** "Historically, battery packs have cost $600 per kilowatt-hour. It's not going to be much better than that in the future. We must accept this cost constraint."

**Reasoning from First Principles:**
1.  **Identify assumptions:** "Batteries will always cost $600/kWh."
2.  **Break down to fundamental principles:** "What are the material constituents of the batteries? What is the stock market value of those materials?" The materials (cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, polymers, and a seal can) cost about $80/kWh on the London Metal Exchange.
3.  **Create new solutions:** Think of clever ways to take those materials and combine them into the shape of a battery cell, drastically reducing the cost of the battery pack and enabling affordable electric vehicles.

## Applying These Examples

When generating solutions using the `first-principles` skill, always strive to emulate this level of deconstruction. Do not accept industry norms or "best practices" if they violate the fundamental truths (the "physics") of the problem.
