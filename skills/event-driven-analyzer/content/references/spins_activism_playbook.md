# Spin-Off, Split-Off, and Activism Playbook

Reference for spin-offs, split-offs, RemainCo / SpinCo trades, activist campaigns, proxy fights, board contests, capital-return campaigns, and strategic-alternative pushes. Two distinct situation families share a playbook because the underwriting both turns on **who is driving change and what they can force**. Organized by driver: issuer-led separation events vs. third-party activist campaigns.

---

## Family A — Issuer-led separation (spin-off / split-off)

### Question to answer

Are SpinCo and RemainCo mispriced because of separation, capital structure, incentives, investor-base rotation, tax constraints, or forced selling?

### Driver type — issuer-led

In a separation, the issuer's board is the change agent. The PM is underwriting how cleanly management can execute, what the post-separation entities are worth standalone, and what technical pressure the distribution creates.

### Required fact pack

- Parent, SpinCo, RemainCo, tickers where available.
- Transaction type: spin, split-off, carve-out, IPO, retained stake, Reverse Morris Trust.
- Record date, distribution date, when-issued trading date.
- Distribution ratio.
- Tax treatment and tax constraints.
- Form 10 / information statement status.
- Pro forma financials by entity.
- Debt allocation and cash transfer.
- Stranded costs, dis-synergies, transition service agreements.
- Management teams and incentives.
- Board composition.
- Retained stake and monetization plan.
- Index eligibility and likely forced selling.
- Liquidity, expected float, short interest / borrow if available.

### Workflow

1. Separate the businesses on paper.
2. Normalize segment financials and pro forma costs.
3. Allocate debt, cash, pensions, environmental, litigation, and tax liabilities.
4. Select peer sets for each entity.
5. Build SOTP value ranges.
6. Analyze technical flows — holder base, index eligibility, size, liquidity, mandate fit.
7. Assess management incentives and capital-allocation posture.
8. Decide entry point: pre-spin, when-issued, post-distribution, or wait.
9. Recommend trade: long parent, long SpinCo, long RemainCo, pair trade, avoid.
10. Build monitoring plan.

### Senior judgment questions

- Does the current shareholder base actually want the spun asset?
- Is SpinCo too small, too levered, too cyclical, too low-margin, or off-index for the natural holder base?
- Are stranded costs understated?
- Is the balance sheet fair, or did the parent over-lever SpinCo?
- Is management incentive alignment real?
- Is this a genuine pure-play re-rating, or conglomerate complexity just relocated?
- Is the better trade pre-spin, in when-issued, or after forced selling?
- Do tax constraints box out strategic alternatives?

### Output requirements

- PM recommendation and timing.
- Spin mechanics and timeline.
- SpinCo valuation range.
- RemainCo valuation range.
- Pro forma capitalization.
- Forced-selling / index-flow assessment.
- Management incentive assessment.
- Trade expression and monitoring plan.

## Family B — Third-party activism

### Question to answer

Can the activist force value creation, board change, sale, breakup, buyback, margin improvement, governance reform, or settlement — and what outcome is priced in?

### Driver type — third-party activist

In activism, the change agent is outside the company. The PM is underwriting credibility, vote math, governance defenses, and how the issuer's response shapes a settlement vs. fight outcome.

### Required fact pack

- Company, ticker, market cap, sector.
- Activist identity, track record, style, prior campaigns.
- Ownership percentage, cost basis where known, derivatives / swaps, filing history.
- Campaign demands.
- Board composition, tenure, independence, committee structure.
- Governance: staggered board, poison pill, bylaws, advance notice, nomination window, universal proxy setup.
- Shareholder base, passive ownership, insider ownership.
- Company response.
- Annual meeting date, record date, nomination deadline.
- ISS / Glass Lewis timing and likely stance if available.
- Prior underperformance and operational issues.
- Value-creation levers: sale, breakup, margin, capital allocation, management change, governance.

### Workflow

1. Identify campaign type: sale process, board refresh, margin plan, capital return, breakup / spin, management change, governance, or strategic review.
2. Assess activist credibility and track record.
3. Build a value-creation bridge — what could move numbers or multiple?
4. Analyze shareholder base and vote math.
5. Assess board vulnerability and defense posture.
6. Estimate settlement probability vs. proxy contest probability.
7. Build scenarios: no change, settlement, partial win, full win, sale, downside.
8. Recommend trade expression and sizing.
9. Build catalyst timeline.

### Senior judgment questions

- Is the activist's plan economically credible, or is it financial engineering?
- Is the board vulnerable on performance, governance, capital allocation, or strategy?
- Will long-only and passive holders support change?
- Is management credible enough to preempt the campaign?
- Is settlement more likely than a full proxy fight?
- What is the downside if the activist loses?
- Does the stock already discount a sale or board change?

### Output requirements

- Campaign summary.
- Activist credibility.
- Value-creation plan assessment.
- Vote math and board vulnerability.
- Settlement / proxy-fight probabilities.
- Expected value by scenario.
- Trade recommendation.
- Next dates and monitoring plan.

## Common mistakes (both families)

- Treating every spin-off as automatically value-creating.
- Ignoring pro forma stranded costs and debt allocation in a spin.
- Assuming forced selling without checking size, index eligibility, holder base, and liquidity.
- Treating activist letters as factual claims rather than advocacy.
- Ignoring bylaws, nomination windows, record dates, and vote math.
- Assuming a sale process is feasible without underwriting buyer universe, antitrust, financing, or board willingness.
