# Litigation and Regulatory Event Playbook

Reference for litigation, regulatory approvals, antitrust review, CFIUS, sector regulators, trials, settlements, appeals, injunctions, damages awards, and agency decisions. Organized **by risk dimension** (merits, timing, remedy, settlement incentive, security translation) rather than by lifecycle stage — these events are rarely linear.

## Boundary — investment vs. legal work

Output is investment analysis, not legal advice. Flag where outside counsel input is required. If the user shares information that might be material non-public information, refuse to base a trade recommendation on it and route them to compliance/legal review.

---

## Part 1 — Litigation events

### What question are we answering?

What is the probability-weighted security impact across the plausible legal outcomes (trial, ruling, settlement, appeal, injunction, damages), and how does timing reshape it?

### Required fact pack

- Company / security.
- Claim type and venue.
- Parties.
- Judge or tribunal where public.
- Procedural posture: complaint, motion to dismiss, discovery, summary judgment, trial, post-trial, appeal, settlement.
- Key dates: hearing, trial, ruling, appeal deadline.
- Damages theory and remedy sought.
- Injunction or business-model impact.
- Company disclosures and reserves where available.
- Insurance, indemnity, or financing capacity where disclosed.
- Prior settlements or comparable cases where available.
- Current market-implied move or damages where it can be inferred.

### Risk dimension 1 — Merits

Decision-relevant question: is the market focused on the right legal question?

Look at:
- The strongest argument on each side.
- Comparable case law and outcomes.
- Likely points of dispute at trial vs. summary judgment.

### Risk dimension 2 — Damages / remedy

Decision-relevant question: how does the cash impact translate to security value?

Look at:
- Cash damages range.
- Royalty / ongoing payments.
- Injunction scope.
- Business-model change.
- Balance-sheet or financing pressure.

### Risk dimension 3 — Timing

Decision-relevant question: is procedural delay an investment risk in itself?

Look at:
- Trial calendar and docket congestion.
- Post-trial / appeal length.
- Settlement-pressure inflection points (e.g., adverse motion outcome, financing need).

### Risk dimension 4 — Settlement incentive

Decision-relevant question: is the event truly binary, or is settlement the actual base case?

Look at:
- Plaintiff motivation (cash, injunction, leverage).
- Defendant willingness and capacity to settle.
- Past settlement patterns in this jurisdiction / industry.

### Risk dimension 5 — Security translation

Decision-relevant question: which security expresses the view most cleanly?

Look at:
- Whether the cash impact is fully reflected in the equity, or whether bonds / CDS / options sit at different probabilities.
- Whether options exposure is cleaner than common given binary outcomes.
- Whether a pair trade isolates the legal driver from market beta.

### Standard litigation workflow

1. State the legal issue in investor language.
2. Lay out procedural posture and timeline.
3. Enumerate outcomes: win, partial loss, loss, injunction, settlement, appeal, delay.
4. Estimate economics for each outcome (cash, royalty, injunction, business-model change, balance-sheet pressure, financing need).
5. Analyze settlement incentives and timing pressure.
6. Read what the market prices in (equity move, options, credit, comparable securities).
7. Build probability tree and expected value.
8. Recommend trade expression: common, options, bonds, pair, avoid, or wait.
9. Build monitoring plan with official sources.

## Part 2 — Regulatory events

### What question are we answering?

Which regulator controls the outcome, what process applies, what remedy or delay is likely, and does the current price compensate for the path risk?

### Required fact pack

- Company / security and the transaction or business event triggering review.
- Regulator(s) and jurisdiction(s).
- Filing status and review stage.
- Statutory or expected timeline.
- Decision standard and core issue.
- Theory of harm / agency concern.
- Customer / competitor / political opposition.
- Remedy feasibility.
- Litigation likelihood.
- Interaction with the outside date, financing, and company liquidity.
- Relevant precedent.

### Regulatory underwriting prompts

Group the analysis by the type of review.

**Antitrust**
- Horizontal concentration and market shares.
- Vertical foreclosure.
- Potential or nascent competition.
- Platform / ecosystem effects.
- Labor-market effects.
- Serial acquisition / roll-up concerns.
- Customer-harm evidence.
- Customer / competitor complaints.
- Divestiture buyer feasibility.
- Behavioral remedy credibility.
- Litigation venue and judge if challenged.

**CFIUS / national security**
- Foreign ownership / control.
- Sensitive personal data exposure.
- Critical technology footprint.
- Critical infrastructure footprint.
- Government contracts.
- Proximity to sensitive facilities.
- Mitigation-agreement feasibility.
- Political environment.
- Interaction with financing and outside date.

**Sector regulators** (banking, utilities, telecom, defense, healthcare, energy, insurance, airlines, …)
- Required approvals.
- Public-interest standard.
- Local / state / federal overlap.
- Ratepayer / customer impact.
- License-transfer issues.
- Foreign-ownership restrictions.
- Political stakeholders.
- Historical approval / remedy pattern.

### Standard regulatory workflow

1. Identify the controlling agency and process.
2. Define the core issue (antitrust, national security, license, safety, data, reimbursement, drug approval, environmental, utility, banking, telecom, defense, foreign ownership).
3. Map official status and likely timeline.
4. Identify remedies, concessions, or conditions that could resolve the issue.
5. Evaluate whether delay alone changes economics.
6. Build outcomes: cleared, cleared with remedy, delayed, challenged / blocked, abandoned, recut.
7. Translate outcomes into security values.
8. Recommend trade expression and monitoring.

### Senior judgment questions

- Where is the real risk — agency review, court litigation, political pressure, or remedy economics?
- Does the outside date allow enough runway?
- Can the buyer accept the remedy without destroying the deal rationale?
- Is the regulatory issue structural, behavioral, national-security, or just process timing?
- Will other jurisdictions coordinate or diverge?
- Is there a credible settlement path?
- What would force a recut or abandonment?

---

## Output requirements (both parts)

- A clear statement of the controlling issue.
- A timeline with known and estimated dates.
- A scenario tree with probabilities and values.
- A remedy / settlement path.
- Market-implied vs. analyst probability where possible.
- Trade expression.
- Monitoring plan tied to official sources.
- Legal-advice caveat where appropriate.
