## Core Capabilities

### 2.1 Academic Paper Formatting

Academic paper formatting is a primary capability of this skill. Different disciplines and journals have unique formatting requirements, and this skill precisely matches specifications to your needs.

**APA Format (American Psychological Association)**: Primarily used for psychology, education, and social sciences. APA format emphasizes the author-date citation system with strict reference formatting requirements, including author names, publication year, article title, journal name, volume/issue, and page numbers. This skill handles APA heading styles, paragraph formatting, in-text citations, and reference list generation automatically.

**MLA Format (Modern Language Association)**: Applied to humanities, languages, literature, and history. MLA uses the author-page citation system with specific guidelines for page setup, title pages, quotations, footnotes/endnotes, and works cited pages. This skill formats according to MLA 9th edition standards.

**GB/T 7714 Format**: China's national standard for bibliographic description, adopted by most Chinese universities and research institutions. GB/T 7714-2015 provides detailed rules for different source types (journal articles, theses, conference papers, patents, standards, reports). This skill automatically applies correct formatting based on source type.

**Chicago Format**: Used primarily in history, art history, and philosophy. Chicago offers two citation styles: Notes and Bibliography and Author-Date. This skill implements either style based on your selection.

## Detailed Formatting Specifications

### 4.1 Academic Paper Font Standards

Academic papers have strict font requirements, typically following these guidelines:

**Body Text**: Song Ti or Times New Roman, size 12pt or 10.5pt. Song Ti for Chinese papers, Times New Roman for English papers. Some disciplines require specific fonts (e.g., Courier for computer science papers).

**Headings**: Chinese papers typically use 16pt bold for level-1 headings, 15pt bold for level-2, 14pt bold for level-3. English paper headings use Arial or Helvetica with progressively smaller sizes. Heading fonts should contrast clearly with body text while maintaining visual harmony.

**Annotation Fonts**: Footnote and endnote text is typically one size smaller than body text (9pt), using the same or contrasting font for distinction.

### 4.2 Paragraph Format Standards

Paragraph formatting directly impacts reading experience:

**First-Line Indent**: Chinese academic papers typically require 2-character indents (~0.74cm) using the dedicated first-line indent function, not manual spaces. English paper indents are typically 0.5 inches (~1.27cm) or left-aligned.

**Line Spacing**: Academic paper body text typically uses 1.5x line spacing for readability and annotation. Some strict guidelines require 0pt before/after paragraphs; others require 6pt spacing to differentiate paragraphs.

**Alignment**: Chinese paper body text is typically justified for clean left-right edges; English papers may use left-aligned or justified based on preference. Headings are typically centered.

### 4.3 Page Format Standards

Page setup is crucial for document formatting:

**Margins**: Chinese academic papers typically require 2.54cm (1 inch) top/bottom and 3.17cm (or 3cm) left/right margins. Margin settings should account for binding—if left-side binding is needed, increase inner margins accordingly.

**Headers and Footers**: Academic paper headers typically display the paper title (left or right) and page numbers. Page numbers are typically centered in footers or bottom-right. This skill guides automatic page numbering setup and different odd/even header/footer content.

**Binding Offset**: For print binding, reserve 1-2cm binding space on the left—achievable by increasing inner margin in page setup.

### 4.4 Citation Format Standards

References are essential components of academic papers; different citation styles have unique rules:

**APA Reference Examples**:

- Journal article: Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. *Journal Name, Volume*(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxxx
- Book: Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of work: Capital letter also for subtitle*. Publisher.

**MLA Works Cited Examples**:

- Journal article: Author Last, First. "Article Title." *Journal Name*, vol. #, no. #, Year, pp. #-#.
- Book: Author Last, First. *Title of Book*. Publisher, Year.

**GB/T 7714 Examples**:

- Journal article: [序号] Author. Title[J]. Journal Name, Year, Vol(Issue): Pages.
- Book: [序号] Author. Book Title[M]. Place: Publisher, Year.

## Important Notes

### 6.1 Version Compatibility

Word documents may have compatibility issues across versions:

**File Format Selection**: Prefer .docx over .doc format—docx is XML-based with better compatibility. When sharing with users on older Word versions, note compatibility save options.

**Feature Limitations**: Some advanced Word features (smart tables, new collaboration features) may be unavailable or display incorrectly in older versions. This skill considers version compatibility when providing guidance.

**Font Alternatives**: If required fonts are unavailable, systems auto-substitute fonts potentially affecting formatting. This skill provides font substitution recommendations.

### 6.2 Format vs. Content Balance

Formatting serves content—avoid compromising clarity for uniformity:

**Flexible Adjustments**: For special cases (overlong formulas, oversized tables), adjust formatting requirements as needed with content clarity as priority.

**Figure Placement**: If figures disrupt readability at current positions, allow captions and figures to separate across pages while avoiding figure/figure-title separation.

**Citation Completeness**: Reference format compliance takes priority over aesthetic appeal—even if formatting seems redundant, ensure complete information.

### 6.3 Common Issue Resolution

**Style Contamination**: Copying from web or other documents may introduce unwanted styles. This skill recommends "paste text only" options, then reapply standard styles.

**TOC Not Updating**: After document edits, TOC doesn't auto-update. Guide users to "Update Table" function for refresh.

**Page Numbering Errors**: Inserting or deleting content causes discontinuous page numbers. Guide users to "Update Field" function to refresh page numbers.

**Section Break Issues**: Improper section break use causes header/footer and page numbering problems. Emphasize cautious section break usage and correct practices.
