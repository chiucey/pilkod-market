# Pyramid Principle Templates

This reference provides practical templates for applying the Pyramid Principle and SCQA framework in various contexts.

## 1. Executive Summary Template (Direct Approach)

Use this for busy executives who want the bottom line immediately.

**[Main Idea / Answer]**
State the core recommendation or conclusion in one clear sentence.

**[Situation]**
Briefly state the current context (1-2 sentences of facts the audience agrees with).

**[Complication]**
State the problem or trigger that requires action.

**[Key Supporting Points]**
- **Point 1**: [Summary of first argument/finding]
- **Point 2**: [Summary of second argument/finding]
- **Point 3**: [Summary of third argument/finding]

---

## 2. Persuasive Argument Template (Indirect Approach)

Use this when the audience needs to be convinced of the problem before hearing the solution.

**[Situation]**
Describe the current state of affairs.

**[Complication]**
Explain what has changed or gone wrong.

**[Question]**
(Implicit or explicit) What should we do about it?

**[Answer / Main Idea]**
Present your solution.

**[Why this works (Key Ideas)]**
- **Reason 1**: [Benefit/Logic]
- **Reason 2**: [Benefit/Logic]
- **Reason 3**: [Benefit/Logic]

---

## 3. Presentation Slide Structure

When creating a slide deck, use the Pyramid Principle to structure the narrative:

- **Slide 1 (Title)**: Clear, action-oriented title.
- **Slide 2 (Executive Summary)**: The SCQA and the Main Idea.
- **Slide 3 (Key Idea 1)**: Headline summarizes the point. Body contains supporting data.
- **Slide 4 (Key Idea 2)**: Headline summarizes the point. Body contains supporting data.
- **Slide 5 (Key Idea 3)**: Headline summarizes the point. Body contains supporting data.
- **Slide 6 (Next Steps)**: Call to action based on the Main Idea.

**Tip**: Slide titles should be full sentences that convey the "so what" of the slide, not just topics (e.g., "Sales declined by 10% due to new competitor" instead of "Q3 Sales Data").
