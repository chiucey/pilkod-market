# RedNote English Viral Copywriting Guide

## Platform Context

RedNote (小红书 / Xiaohongshu) English content targets bilingual/international audiences who appreciate aesthetic lifestyle content. The tone blends authenticity, aspiration, and relatability — it feels personal, not corporate.

---

## Title Formula (Hook Titles)

A viral title must hook the reader within 2 seconds. Use one or more of these proven patterns:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Number + Benefit** | "5 reasons this serum changed my skin forever" |
| **Secret/Hidden Gem** | "The lipstick shade nobody talks about but everyone needs" |
| **Relatable Pain Point** | "Oily skin girlies, this one's for you 🙌" |
| **Before/After Promise** | "I tried this for 7 days and here's what happened..." |
| **Question Hook** | "Why does everyone in Seoul carry this bag?" |
| **Hype Statement** | "This is NOT a drill — the best moisturizer of 2025 ✨" |
| **Exclusive/Discovered** | "Found the most underrated cafe snack and I'm obsessed" |

**Title rules:**
- Keep it under 20 words
- Use 1–2 emojis max (don't overdo it)
- Include the product category naturally
- Avoid generic words like "amazing", "great", "good"

## Body Copy Structure

### Opening Line (Hook — 1–2 sentences)
Start with a personal story, bold claim, or relatable scenario. Make the reader feel seen.

> "Okay girlies, I've been sitting on this review for TWO weeks because I needed to be sure..."
> "Real talk — I've wasted so much money on foundations that oxidize. Until now."

### Product Introduction (2–3 sentences)
Naturally introduce the product. Mention key details: name, what it is, and the standout feature.

> "This is the [Product Name] — a [brief description] that [core benefit]. What makes it different is [unique selling point]."

### Experience / Story Section (3–5 sentences)
Tell a mini story about using the product. Be specific with sensory details and real feelings.

> "The texture is like [analogy]. When I first tried it, [specific reaction]. After [timeframe], I noticed [tangible result]."

### Key Benefits Breakdown
Use bullet points or line breaks for scannability. Focus on 3–5 genuine benefits.

> ✨ [Benefit 1]
> 💧 [Benefit 2]
> 🔥 [Benefit 3]

### Call to Action / Closing (1–2 sentences)
End with a warm recommendation or question to drive engagement.

> "If you're on the fence, just go for it — your future self will thank you 💕"
> "Drop a 🙋 if you want me to do a full week review!"

## Viral Language Patterns

### Power Phrases (use sparingly, 1–2 per post)
- "not sponsored, just obsessed"
- "girlies / besties / babes" (community address)
- "we need to talk about..."
- "the way I immediately..."
- "rent free in my head"
- "running out of my 3rd bottle"
- "I don't make the rules"
- "doing it for the plot"
- "core aesthetic"
- "main character energy"

### Emoji Usage Strategy
- Use emojis as **visual bullets** or **emotional punctuation**, not decoration
- Recommended: ✨ 💕 🙌 💧 🔥 👀 💯 🫶 🌿 ✅
- Place emojis at the **end of a sentence** or **start of bullet points**
- Max 8–10 emojis per full post

## Length Guidelines

| Section | Word Count |
|---|---|
| Title | 8–20 words |
| Body (short post) | 80–150 words |
| Body (standard post) | 150–250 words |
| Body (detailed review) | 250–400 words |

Total post (title + body + hashtags) should feel **easy to scan** — use line breaks generously.

---

## Things to Avoid

- ❌ Corporate/ad-speak ("innovative solution", "cutting-edge technology")
- ❌ Hyperbole without substance ("THE BEST EVER!!!")
- ❌ Wall of text — always use line breaks
- ❌ Generic hashtags with 100M+ posts (too noisy)
- ❌ Spelling out "hashtag" or explaining the post
- ❌ Ending with "Follow for more!" (feels spammy)
