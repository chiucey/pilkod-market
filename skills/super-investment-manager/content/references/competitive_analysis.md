# Porter Five Forces Competitive Analysis Framework

## Five Forces Model Introduction

Porter's Five Forces model was proposed by Michael Porter in 1979 to analyze industry competitive landscape and attractiveness.

## Five Forces Analysis Dimensions

### 1. Bargaining Power of Suppliers

**Assessment Questions**:
- Is the number of suppliers limited?
- Do suppliers control key resources?
- Do alternative suppliers exist?
- What's the possibility of supplier forward integration?

**Signal Identification**:
- Raw material prices rising
- Suppliers reducing credit terms
- Tight supply of key components

### 2. Bargaining Power of Buyers

**Assessment Questions**:
- Is customer concentration high?
- What is the degree of product differentiation?
- Are customer switching costs low?
- What's the possibility of buyer backward integration?

**Signal Identification**:
- Major customers demanding price reductions
- Customer churn rate increasing
- Gross margin continuously declining

### 3. Threat of New Entrants

**Assessment Questions**:
- How high are the barriers to entry?
  - Economies of scale
  - Brand loyalty
  - Capital requirements
  - Sales channels
  - Government policy
- What are the resources and capabilities of new entrants?

**Signal Identification**:
- New players announcing entry
- Tech giants expanding into the space
- Policy liberalization introducing competition

### 4. Threat of Substitutes

**Assessment Questions**:
- What is the price and performance of substitutes?
- What is the market penetration rate of substitutes?
- What is the cost for users to switch to substitutes?

**Signal Identification**:
- Substitutes achieving technological breakthrough
- Substitute prices declining
- User usage habits changing

### 5. Competitive Rivalry

**Assessment Questions**:
- How many and how strong are competitors?
- What is the industry growth situation?
- What is the degree of product homogeneity?
- How high are the exit barriers?

**Signal Identification**:
- Price wars occurring frequently
- Marketing expenses surging
- Industry profit margins declining

## Investment Decision Application

### High-Attractiveness Industry Characteristics
- Total Five Forces score >= 18
- High entry barriers
- Low substitute threat
- Relatively mild industry competition

### Low-Attractiveness Industry Characteristics
- Total Five Forces score < 12
- Frequent price wars
- Many new entrants
- Strong substitute capability

## Precautions

1. **Dynamic Analysis**: Five Forces structure changes over time
2. **Combined Analysis**: Various forces interact with each other
3. **Regional Differences**: Five Forces structure differs in different regional markets
4. **Industry Segmentation**: Different market segments within the same large industry may have significant differences
