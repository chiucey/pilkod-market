# Best Practices — Earnings Update

## Headline Writing

**GOOD headlines:**
- "Revenue Beats by 7% on Strong Enterprise Demand; Raising PT to $XX"
- "Q4 Results In-Line: Margin Pressure Offset by Solid Cloud Growth"
- "ATTC Platform Data Strong but Regulatory Path Uncertain; Maintaining HOLD"

**BAD headlines:**
- "Company Reports Q3 Results" ❌ (too vague, no key info)
- "Great Quarter for Everyone" ❌ (unprofessional, no specifics)
- "Strong Performance but Challenges Remain" ❌ (worthless filler)

## Rating / PT Change Protocol

**Always explain WHY you are changing a rating or price target.**

❌ BAD: "Raising PT to $XX" (no explanation)
✅ GOOD: "Raising PT to $XX on revised EBITDA estimates and the recent Servier BD deal derisking the pipeline. Our new PT implies 18x FY2026E EV/EBITDA, in line with specialty pharma peers."

**If rating is unchanged:**
- State it: "Rating MAINTAINED at BUY"
- Give one sentence on why: "Despite Q4 deceleration, the AI-native product momentum and >70% overseas revenue justify maintaining our constructive view."

## What Makes a Great Earnings Update

### ✅ DO

1. **Lead with the investment conclusion** — what did the quarter say about the thesis?
2. **Quantify everything** — "beat by $XXM" not "beat expectations"
3. **Explain the "why"** — not just what happened but what drove it
4. **Be opinionated** — a sell-side report without a view is useless
5. **Be timely** — publish within 24-48 hours of earnings
6. **Distinguish operational vs. accounting items** — e.g., SBC from IPO is one-time
7. **Contextualize against consensus** — cite the consensus source and date
8. **Update the model** — estimates are the core deliverable
9. **Flag what's NOT in the release** — silence on key topics is itself information
10. **Be direct about risk** — don't bury it in small print

### ❌ DON'T

1. **Rehash company background** — reader has seen the initiation
2. **Dump full financial statements** — only highlight metrics
3. **Use vague language** — "strong performance" means nothing without numbers
4. **Skip the sources** — every claim needs a citation
5. **Include plain text URLs** — hyperlink properly
6. **Forget to update the price target** — always revisit after results
7. **Treat all metrics equally** — focus on the 2-3 that actually matter to the thesis
8. **Make it too long** — 8-12 pages max; 20 pages means you don't know what's important
9. **Ignore deceleration signals** — flag them early
10. **Be afraid to change your view** — new data should sometimes change your model

## Quality Checklist

### Content
- [ ] Headline captures the key takeaway
- [ ] Executive summary has 3-5 bullet takeaways (max 5 lines each)
- [ ] Beat/miss analysis on every key metric
- [ ] At least one paragraph explaining WHY results differed from expectations
- [ ] Segment/geographic/margin breakdown included
- [ ] Updated guidance analyzed (changed/ maintained/ withdrawn)
- [ ] At least 2 paragraphs on thesis implications
- [ ] New information explicitly connected to thesis
- [ ] Key risks flagged (including risks NOT mentioned in earnings release)
- [ ] All claims quantified with specific numbers

### Sources & Citations
- [ ] Every figure has source footnote
- [ ] Every table has source reference
- [ ] All URLs are clickable hyperlinks (not plain text)
- [ ] SEC filings linked to EDGAR
- [ ] Consensus source cited with date
- [ ] Earnings release date confirmed
- [ ] Transcript date confirmed

### Formatting
- [ ] Font: Times New Roman throughout
- [ ] Report length: 8-12 pages
- [ ] 8-12 charts included
- [ ] 1-3 tables only (not comprehensive)
- [ ] Word count: 3,000-5,000
- [ ] Sources section at end with all URLs
- [ ] Page margins: 1 inch all sides
- [ ] Chart footnotes: Times New Roman 8pt

### Estimates
- [ ] FY current year estimates updated
- [ ] FY+1 estimates updated
- [ ] Prior vs. new estimates side by side
- [ ] Key model assumption changes explained

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: "The Kitchen Sink" Report
**Problem:** Including everything = nothing stands out
**Fix:** Ask "so what?" for every paragraph. If the answer is "not much," cut it.

### Mistake 2: Forgetting to Update the PT
**Problem:** PT stale after major move
**Fix:** Always revisit PT after earnings. Update it or explicitly confirm it is unchanged and why.

### Mistake 3: Missing the "Why"
**Problem:** "Revenue +15% YoY" without explaining the driver
**Fix:** Every revenue/margin figure needs a 1-sentence driver explanation.

### Mistake 4: Using Training Data Instead of Live Search
**Problem:** Citing a Q3 2024 transcript when it's now Q1 2026
**Fix:** Before starting, always search for the latest. Write down today's date and verify the release is recent.

### Mistake 5: hyperlinks as Plain Text
**Problem:** `https://www.sec.gov/...` appears as plain URL, not clickable
**Fix:** In DOCX, select the display text → Insert → Hyperlink → paste URL. The link must show as blue underlined text.

### Mistake 6: No Thesis Connection
**Problem:** Great operational data but no discussion of what it means for the investment case
**Fix:** Every earnings update should answer: "Does this change our thesis, and how?"

### Mistake 7: Ignoring Deceleration
**Problem:** Reporting "revenue +20% YoY" without flagging it was +35% last quarter
**Fix:** Always show YoY AND QoQ growth. Flag acceleration AND deceleration.

| Orange | #ED7D31 | Mixed results, caution |
| Red | #C00000 | Negative variance, misses |

## File Naming Convention

```
[Company]_[Ticker]_Q[X]_[Year]_Earnings_Update.docx
```

Examples:
- `MiniMax_03696.HK_Q4_2025_Earnings_Update.docx`
- `HutchMed_00013.HK_Q4_2025_Earnings_Update.docx`
- `SUNeVision_01686.HK_FY_2025_Earnings_Update.docx`

**If revised (after initial publication):**
- `Company_Ticker_Q4_2025_Earnings_Update_v2.docx`
- Or add date: `Company_Ticker_Q4_2025_Earnings_Update_2026-03-05.docx`
