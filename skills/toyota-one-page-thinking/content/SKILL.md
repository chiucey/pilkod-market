# Toyota One-Page Thinking (丰田一页纸极简思考法)

This skill provides a structured methodology for organizing information and clarifying thoughts using the "One-Page" approach developed at Toyota. The core philosophy is that any complex information or problem can be summarized on a single piece of paper to facilitate understanding, communication, and action.

## Core Principles

1. **Think for the Reader**: Always organize information with the assumption that you will explain it to someone else. The goal is to make the reader understand instantly and take the desired action.
2. **Clarify the Purpose**: Before writing, clearly define the purpose of the document and the target audience.
3. **Action First (ACTION FIRST!)**: Do not get stuck overthinking. Start writing and filling in the blanks. The act of writing itself helps organize your thoughts.
4. **Visual Clarity (High "Face Value")**: A good one-page document has three elements:
   - **At-a-glance visibility (一览性)**: The entire structure is visible at once.
   - **Framework (框架)**: Information is categorized into tables or grids.
   - **Themes (主题)**: Each section has a clear heading or theme.

## The Two Core Frameworks

This methodology relies on two primary frameworks: **Excel1** (for organizing information and thoughts) and **Logic3** (for structuring communication logically).

### 1. Excel1 (Information & Thought Organization)

Use Excel1 when you have scattered information, need to brainstorm, prioritize tasks, or prepare for a meeting.

**How to use Excel1:**
1. **Create the Grid**: Draw a grid (e.g., 8, 16, or 32 boxes) on a single page.
2. **Set Date and Theme**: Write the date and the specific theme/question in the top-left box (e.g., "What are the key points of today's meeting?", "Tasks for today").
3. **Fill the Boxes (Blue Pen Phase)**: Rapidly fill the boxes with keywords or short phrases related to the theme. One idea per box. Do this quickly (1-3 minutes) without overthinking.
4. **Organize and Prioritize (Red Pen Phase)**: Review the filled boxes and identify the most important items (usually the top 3). Circle them, number them by priority, or categorize them using symbols (e.g., ○ for important, △ for urgent).

### 2. Logic3 (Logical Communication)

Use Logic3 when you need to present information, write a report, or explain a concept clearly to others. It ensures your communication is logical and addresses the reader's needs.

**How to use Logic3:**
1. **Create the Framework**: Draw a grid with spaces for a Date/Theme, a summary sentence (1P?), and three key questions (Q1?, Q2?, Q3?) with their corresponding answers.
2. **Define the Theme**: Write the date and theme in the top-left corner.
3. **Formulate the 3 Questions (Green Pen Phase)**: Think about what the reader wants or needs to know. Formulate three questions (Q1?, Q2?, Q3?). A good starting point is the What/How/Why framework:
   - Q1?: What is the current situation/problem?
   - Q2?: How was it resolved / What are the details?
   - Q3?: Why did it happen / What is the future plan?
4. **Answer the Questions (Blue Pen Phase)**: Provide concise answers to the three questions (aim for 3 points max per answer).
5. **Write the 1-Phrase Summary (Red Pen Phase)**: Summarize the entire document in one clear, compelling sentence (1P?).

## Application Guide for Manus

When a user asks you to summarize a document, prepare a report, organize tasks, or clarify a complex topic using the "Toyota One-Page" or "极简思考法" method, follow these steps:

1. **Identify the Goal**: Determine if the user needs to brainstorm/organize (use Excel1) or communicate/report (use Logic3).
2. **Execute the Framework**:
   - For **Excel1**: Generate a markdown table representing the grid. Clearly state the Theme. Fill the cells with relevant extracted information. Explicitly mark the top 3 priorities or key insights (e.g., using bold text or emojis like 🔴).
   - For **Logic3**: Generate a structured output containing:
     - **Theme**: The topic.
     - **1P (1-Phrase Summary)**: The core message.
     - **Q1, Q2, Q3**: The three critical questions anticipated from the reader's perspective.
     - **Answers**: Concise bullet points answering each question.
3. **Format for Clarity**: Ensure the final output is presented as a single, highly readable markdown block (the "One Page"), utilizing tables, bold text, and clear headings to achieve "At-a-glance visibility."
