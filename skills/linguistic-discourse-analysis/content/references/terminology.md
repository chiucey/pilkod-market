# Linguistic Discourse Analysis - Reference Guide

## Key Theoretical Frameworks

### Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL) - Halliday

SFL views language as a social semiotic system with three metafunctions:

1. **Ideational function**: Language as a means of representing experience
2. **Interpersonal function**: Language as action and interaction
3. **Textual function**: Language as creating coherent text

### Discourse Analysis Traditions

| Tradition | Key Scholars | Focus |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Firthian/SFL | Halliday, Hasan | Language as social meaning system |

## Detailed Cohesion Categories

### Reference (照应) - Examples

**Personal Reference**:
- First person: I, we, my, our, us
- Second person: you, your, yours
- Third person: he, she, it, they, his, her, its, their

**Demonstrative Reference**:
- This/these (proximal - near speaker)
- That/those (distal - away from speaker)

**Comparative Reference**:
- Same, identical, equal, similar, different, other, better, more

### Conjunction Types

**Additive**:
- Simple: and, also, too
- Contrastive: and yet, but also

**Adversative**:
- Contrastive: but, yet, however, though, although
- Corrective: instead, rather, on the contrary

**Causal**:
- Cause: because, for, since
- Effect: so, therefore, consequently, thus, hence
- Condition: if, unless, provided that

**Temporal**:
- Then, next, after that, finally, meanwhile, previously

### Lexical Cohesion - Reiteration Types

**Repetition**: Same word repeated
**Synonymy**: Near-equivalent words (big/large, happy/glad)
**Antonymy**: Opposite meaning (hot/cold, before/after)
**Hyponymy**: Subordinate-superordinate (dog/animal)
**Meronymy**: Part-whole (finger/hand)
**Collocation**: Words that co-occur (doctor/patient, hospital/nurse)

## Theme Types in English

### Unmarked Theme (default)
> **The cat** sat on the mat.
- Subject = Topical theme (default)

### Marked Theme (non-subject first position)
> **Slowly** the door opened.
> **On the table** sat a vase.

### Multiple Theme Structure
> **Well, honestly, probably** the manager himself will not agree.

| Element | Example | Function |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Textual | and, but, so, well | Connect to previous |
| Interpersonal | probably, unfortunately, I think | Express stance |
| Topical | Subject, complement, adjunct | Message content |

## CDA Key Concepts

### Power Relations
- **Euphemism**: Hiding unpleasant truths (collateral damage for civilian casualties)
- **Dysphemism**: Making things sound worse (terrorist vs. freedom fighter)
- **Hedges**: Distancing from claims (may, might, possibly)
- **Boosters**: Emphasizing certainty (definitely, clearly)

### Ideological Square (van Dijk)
1. Emphasize positive IN-group / negative OUT-group
2. De-emphasize negative IN-group / positive OUT-group
3. Hide in-group bad / hide out-group good

### Common Ideological Markers
- **Agent deletion**: Passive constructions hide who did what
- **Nominalization**: Turning processes into "things"
- **Presupposition**: Embedding assumptions as facts
- **Connotation**: Words with emotional associations

## Move Structure Examples

### Research Article Introduction (Swales' CARS)

**Move 1: Establishing a territory**
- Claim about current state of knowledge
- Topic generalizations

**Move 2: Establishing a niche**
- Gap indicator: "however", "few studies"
- Problem indicator: "unclear", "controversial"
- Need indicator: "important to", "necessary"

**Move 3: Occupying the niche**
- Outline solution/approach
- State research purpose
- Announce findings (optional)

### News Report Structure
1. Lead/Headline (most newsworthy element)
2. Elaboration (background context)
3. Attribution (sources)
4. History (how we got here)
5. Context (wider implications)

## Practical Analysis Tips

### Identifying Register
Ask:
- What field/topics are discussed? (technical vs. everyday)
- What is the relationship between participants? (power, distance)
- What mode/channel is used? (written, spoken, digital)

### Counting Themes
1. Mark each clause
2. Identify first constituent = Theme
3. Categorize: Textual / Interpersonal / Topical
4. Calculate markedness ratio

### Analyzing Thematic Progression
**Linear**: Each Rheme becomes next Theme
**Constant**: Same Theme develops
**Derived**: Themes derive from a hypertheme

### Critical Reading Questions
1. Who constructed this text? For whom?
2. What is left unsaid? What is taken for granted?
3. Who benefits from this framing?
4. What alternative framings are possible?
5. How are "we" and "they" constructed?
