# Linguistic Discourse Analysis

Systematic linguistic analysis of texts/discourse using established frameworks from systemic functional linguistics (SFL), discourse analysis, and critical discourse analysis.

## Analysis Framework

### 1. Basic Text Information
- **Text Type**: Identify genre/register (news article, academic text, advertisement, conversation, etc.)
- **Topic/Field**: Subject matter, domain, and specialized terminology
- **Participants**: Writer/speaker and intended audience relationship
- **Medium**: Written/spoken, monologic/dialogic, channel of communication

### 2. Register Analysis (Halliday's Three Variables)

**Field (语场)**: What is being discussed
- Subject domain and specialization level
- Technical vs. layperson vocabulary
- Action/process descriptions vs. evaluation/classification

**Tenor (语旨)**: Who is involved and their relationships
- Social distance between participants
- Power relations (formal/informal, hierarchical/equal)
- Emotional stance (neutral, passionate, detached)

**Mode (语式)**: How the text is organized
- Written vs. spoken mode
- Monologic vs. dialogic
- Channel: print, digital, face-to-face, phone, etc.

### 3. Cohesion Analysis

**Grammatical Cohesion**:
- **Reference (照应)**: Personal (he/she/they), demonstrative (this/that), comparative (same/different)
- **Substitution (替代)**: one, do, not, etc.
- **Ellipsis (省略)**: Omission of recoverable elements
- **Conjunction (连接)**: Additive (and, moreover), adversative (but, however), causal (because, therefore), temporal (then, next)

**Lexical Cohesion (词汇衔接)**:
- **Reiteration**: Repetition, synonymy, hyponymy, meronymy
- **Collocation**: Semantic association between words

### 4. Thematic Structure Analysis

**Theme (主位)**: The point of departure - what the message is about
- **Topical theme**: The subject/entity being discussed
- **Interpersonal theme**: Modal verbs, vocatives, attitudinal adjuncts
- **Textual theme**: Conjunctions, continuity markers

**Rheme (述位)**: The development/new information

Calculate:
- **Theme markedness**: Ratio of marked (non-subject) themes
- **Thematic progression patterns**: Linear, constant, derived

### 5. Information Structure

- **Given vs. New information**: What is presupposed vs. newly introduced
- **Focus**: Emphatic elements, contrastive stress
- **End-focus principle**: Important information tends to appear at the end

### 6. Discourse Markers & Metadiscourse

**Discourse Markers**:
- Framing markers (first, finally, in conclusion)
- Evidential markers (according to, as stated)
- Code glosses (namely, that is, for example)

**Interpersonal Metadiscourse**:
- Hedges (may, might, possibly)
- Boosters (clearly, definitely, certainly)
- Attitude markers (unfortunately, surprisingly)

### 7. Genre/Move Structure Analysis (Swalesian CARS Model)

For academic/research texts:
- **Move 1**: Establishing a territory (claims, background)
- **Move 2**: Establishing a niche (gap, problem)
- **Move 3**: Occupying the niche (solution, findings)

For other genres, identify:
- Conventional stages and their sequencing
- Optional vs. obligatory moves
- Rhetorical purpose of each move

### 8. Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) Dimensions

**Three-dimensional model (Fairclough)**:
1. **Text**: Linguistic features (vocabulary, grammar, metaphors)
2. **Discourse practice**: Production and consumption context
3. **Socio-cultural practice**: Broader power relations and ideologies

**Questions to address**:
- How is social power exercised through this text?
- What ideologies are naturalized or challenged?
- How are identities constructed?
- Whose interests are served?

## Analysis Guidelines

1. **Adapt depth to purpose**: Deep analysis for academic purposes; focused analysis for specific questions
2. **Support claims with examples**: Always cite specific text segments
3. **Connect form to function**: Explain WHY linguistic choices matter
4. **Consider context**: Political, cultural, situational factors influence interpretation
5. **Distinguish description from interpretation**: Report objective features first, then offer interpretations
6. **Acknowledge limitations**: Alternative interpretations are valid
