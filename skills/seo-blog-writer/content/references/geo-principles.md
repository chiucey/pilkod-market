# GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) Principles

GEO is the practice of optimizing content so it gets cited, quoted, or surfaced by
AI-powered search engines: Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, Bing Copilot,
Claude, and similar systems.

---

## Why GEO Matters for B2B Content

AI engines are becoming a primary research tool for B2B buyers. Being cited in an AI
answer is the new "featured snippet" — it drives brand awareness, trust, and referral
traffic even when the user doesn't click through. B2B buyers often validate vendors via
AI research before engaging.

---

## The 7 Core GEO Principles

### 1. Definitional Clarity
AI engines love clear, citable definitions. Structure key definitions like:
> "[Term] is [concise definition]. It works by [mechanism]. It matters because [value]."

Write at least one clear definition per major concept. Put it early in the relevant section.

### 2. Factual Density with Attribution
AI systems prefer content with verifiable facts. Include:
- Specific statistics (not vague claims like "many companies")
- Source attribution inline: "According to [Source], X% of..."
- Dates on all statistics (AI systems flag outdated data)
- Primary sources preferred (Gartner, McKinsey, industry associations, peer-reviewed)

### 3. Direct Answer Format
Answer questions directly before expanding. AI engines scan for the answer, then the
explanation. Structure like:
> Q: What is X? → Answer in 1–2 sentences. Then elaborate.

Use this pattern especially in FAQ sections.

### 4. Structured, Scannable Formats
AI engines extract structured content better than prose paragraphs:
- Numbered steps for processes
- Bullet lists for features, benefits, comparisons
- Tables for comparisons, data, feature matrices
- Bold text for key terms (but use sparingly)

### 5. Authority Signals
AI systems assess trustworthiness. Boost authority signals with:
- Citing named experts or studies
- Including original data or proprietary insights
- Mentioning specific frameworks, methodologies, or named best practices
- Publishing bylines with credentials (in CMS — not in article copy itself)

### 6. Comprehensive Coverage
AI engines prefer content that thoroughly covers a topic over thin content that just
mentions a topic. Ensure:
- Every logical subtopic is addressed
- Common follow-up questions are pre-answered (use FAQ for this)
- Edge cases and "it depends" scenarios are acknowledged

### 7. Semantic Richness
Use topically related vocabulary throughout the article. If writing about "B2B lead
generation", naturally include: pipeline, MQL, SQL, conversion rate, demand generation,
inbound, account-based marketing, etc. This signals topical authority to both Google
and AI engines.

## FAQ Section Best Practices for GEO

The FAQ section is the single highest-leverage GEO element. AI engines frequently
excerpt FAQ answers verbatim.

Format each FAQ entry like:
```
**Q: [Question phrased as a user would actually ask it]**

[Topic] is/works by/means... [Direct 2–4 sentence answer that stands alone without
needing the rest of the article for context]. [Optional: one specific example or stat].
```

Choose FAQ questions by asking: "What would someone type into Perplexity or
ChatGPT after reading this article?" — use those questions.

---

## Content Structures That Get Cited Most Often

1. **Step-by-step processes** ("How to X in N steps")
2. **Comparison tables** ("X vs. Y: [feature comparison]")
3. **Definition + explanation** ("What is X? X is...")
4. **Statistics roundups** with sources
5. **Named frameworks** (give your advice a name, e.g., "The RACE Framework")
6. **Numbered lists with explanations** (not just bullet points)

---

## What to Avoid for GEO

- Vague claims without data ("many companies find that...")
- Passive voice overuse (harder for AI to extract clear subjects)
- Very long paragraphs with multiple ideas (AI struggles to isolate the key claim)
- Excessive hedging ("it might be possible that...") — be assertive and citable
- Duplicate/boilerplate intros that don't contain unique information
