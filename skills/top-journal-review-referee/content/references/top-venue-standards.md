# Top-Venue Standards

Use this reference to map a review manuscript to the expectations of high-level journals. The point is not to mimic house style; it is to test whether the manuscript clears the venue's intellectual bar.

## Cross-Venue Editorial Questions

Ask these before line editing:

1. Why this review now?
2. What is the central organizing framework?
3. What will a strong reader understand differently after reading it?
4. Which important literatures are intentionally omitted, and is that defensible?
5. Does the manuscript synthesize evidence, or does it list papers?
6. Are controversies, failures, uncertainties, and boundary conditions made visible?
7. Are display items doing intellectual work?
8. Is the review useful to adjacent experts, not only insiders?
9. Is the reference list current, balanced, selective, and not dominated by the authors' own work?
10. Does the conclusion give a research agenda, not just a summary?

## Nature, Nature portfolio, and Nature Communications

Editorial signal:
- Nature Portfolio peer review emphasizes advance in understanding, strength of evidence, originality/significance, appropriate references, clarity/context, and conclusions supported by interpretation.
- Nature journals often desk-reject solid papers if the advance is too specialist, insufficiently novel, or lacks conceptual reach.
- Nature Communications evaluates novelty, potential impact, scope fit, conceptual or methodological advance, and readership interest.

Review-manuscript implication:
- Make the opening establish a field-level problem, not only a domain trend.
- State the article's conceptual advance early: taxonomy, framework, roadmap, explanatory synthesis, or decision architecture.
- Ensure the manuscript has a discernible reason to deserve a high-visibility venue rather than a specialist journal.

- Nature Communications guide: https://www.nature.com/ncomms/submit/guide-to-authors

## Nature Reviews and Nature Reviews Earth & Environment

Editorial signal:
- Nature Reviews articles should be authoritative, accessible, balanced, selective, and interpretive.
- They target specialists and advanced non-specialists.
- Authors are expected to discuss implications rather than simply describe findings.
- Proposals often include a short abstract, main sections, key references, and authorship details.

Review-manuscript implication:
- The introduction must explain importance and timeliness, then end with a guiding paragraph.
- The body should select and interpret the most important work.
- The conclusion should cover implications and future directions.
- Review-type content should not include unpublished primary research.

Useful sources:
- Nature Reviews Earth & Environment preparation: http://feeds.nature.com/natrevearthenviron/for-authors/preparing-your-submission
- Nature Reviews review format guide: https://www.nature.com/documents/natrev-articleformatguide-review.pdf
- Nature Reviews perspective format guide: https://www.nature.com/reviews/pdf/perspective-ps.pdf

## Nature Machine Intelligence

Editorial signal:
- Reviews are authoritative, balanced, scholarly surveys of recent developments.
- Reviews should be broad enough not to be dominated by one laboratory or the authors' own work.
- Perspectives may be more opinionated and forward-looking but must remain balanced.
- Illustrations are encouraged; references are selective.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For spatiotemporal intelligent perception, foundation models, autonomous sensing, or UAV intelligence, test whether the manuscript speaks to machine intelligence as a field, not only one application.
- Make controversies and limitations visible: data bias, domain shift, robustness, physical constraints, safety, interpretability, and deployment.

Useful sources:
- Nature Machine Intelligence content types: https://www.nature.com/natmachintell/content
- Nature Machine Intelligence submission guidelines: https://www.nature.com/natmachintell/submission-guidelines/

## Communications Engineering

Editorial signal:
- Publishes high-quality research, reviews, and commentary across engineering.
- Reviews should inform non-specialist readers while remaining scholarly for specialists.
- Review scope should not be dominated by a single laboratory or authors' own work.
- Criteria include novelty, strong evidence, technical soundness, and importance to the engineering subdiscipline.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For low-altitude systems and route networks, emphasize engineering opportunity, capability, benefit, field studies, system-level implications, and practical constraints.
- Avoid purely algorithmic framing unless it changes engineering capability.

Useful sources:
- Aims and scope: https://www.nature.com/commseng/aims
- Content types: https://www.nature.com/commseng/submit/content-types
- Guide to authors: http://www.npg.nature.com/commseng/submit/guide-to-authors

## Science

Editorial signal:
- Science Reviews should be novel, insightful, and authoritative syntheses of fields showing wide-reaching progress over the past 3-5 years.
- Reviews should not be workshop summaries and should not contain unpublished data.
- Science encourages author-produced schematic figures.
- Self-citation should be restrained.

Review-manuscript implication:
- A Science-style review must show why the field is at an inflection point.
- The title, abstract, and figures must communicate to a broad scientific audience.
- The article should extract cross-field lessons, not simply summarize one technical niche.

Useful source:
- Science information for authors: https://www.science.org/content/page/science-information-authors

## ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing

Editorial signal:
- Official ISPRS journal for photogrammetry, remote sensing, spatial information systems, computer vision, and related fields.
- Encourages broad scientific interest, innovative applications, interdisciplinary work, emerging topics, and new directions.
- Review papers are extensive state-of-the-art surveys of established or emerging topics or application areas.
- Perspective papers should provide vision, suggest directions, detect trends, or synthesize advances across subfields.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For UAV photogrammetry and remote sensing, show both theoretical background and system/application relevance.
- A review should connect sensors, geometry, algorithms, systems, experiments, and applications.
- Avoid a single-method survey without a field-level synthesis.

Useful sources:
- Journal page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/isprs-journal-of-photogrammetry-and-remote-sensing
- Elsevier shop aims/scope summary: https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/isprs-journal-of-photogrammetry-and-remote-sensing/0924-2716

## Remote Sensing of Environment

Editorial signal:
- RSE focuses on innovative methods and applications of remotely sensed data.
- Emphasis is on biophysical and quantitative approaches from local to global scales.
- Review articles should synthesize beyond literature compilation and provide insights into trends.
- For research papers, rigorous accuracy assessment or validation is central; reviews should still treat validation and uncertainty seriously.

Review-manuscript implication:
- If targeting RSE, make the environmental or Earth-observation contribution explicit.
- Do not frame the manuscript as pure algorithm development.
- Discuss validation, transferability, scale, uncertainty, and application consequences.

Useful source:
- Guide for authors: https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/remote-sensing-of-environment/publish/guide-for-authors

## IEEE TGRS and IEEE JSTARS

Editorial signal:
- TGRS focuses on theory, concepts, techniques, sensing instruments, processing, interpretation, and dissemination of geoscientific information.
- Experimental apparatus, methods, data, and conditions must be complete enough for technical evaluation.
- JSTARS emphasizes applied remote and in situ sensing, data integration, applied modeling, and Earth information creation.
- Both expect clear English, accessible terminology, and strong illustrations.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For IEEE remote-sensing venues, foreground methodological novelty, taxonomy rigor, dataset/benchmark comparability, experimental reproducibility, and technical completeness.
- Explain terminology for readers across remote-sensing subfields.

Useful sources:
- TGRS scope: https://www.grss-ieee.org/publications/transactions-on-geoscience-remote-sensing/
- TGRS author information: https://www.grss-ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Information-for-Authors_TGRS_pdf.pdf
- JSTARS author information: https://www.grss-ieee.org/publications/jstars-information-for-authors/

## IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Magazine

Editorial signal:
- GRSM is aimed at the broad GRSS "community of communities" and publishes reviews, summaries, tutorials, and selected technical papers.
- Feature articles should be informational, inclusive of multiple perspectives, and not primarily new technical results.
- Tutorial and review papers are first proposed as a white paper; the white paper must clearly identify whether the article is a tutorial, review, or special issue paper and justify fit and quality.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For UAV photogrammetry and remote sensing, GRSM-style work should teach the field and clarify emerging trends, not compete with TGRS on narrow methodological novelty.
- Make the reader journey explicit: background, terminology, representative methods, comparison, pitfalls, open problems, and practical guidance.

Useful sources:
- GRSM publication page: https://www.grss-ieee.org/publications/geoscience-and-remote-sensing-magazine/
- GRSM instructions: https://www.grss-ieee.org/instructions-for-authors/

## IEEE T-ITS and Transportation Research Part C

Editorial signal:
- IEEE T-ITS covers sensing, communications, controls, planning, design, implementation, modeling, simulation, optimization, AI, multi-agent systems, and transportation networks, but the transportation-system connection must be explicit.
- Transportation Research Part C focuses on transportation implications of emerging technologies, not technologies in isolation.
- TR-C is interested in planning, design, operation, control, safety, reliability, resource consumption, environment, and large-scale datasets.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For low-altitude route networks and aerial transportation systems, do not let the paper become a drone-technology survey.
- Make the transportation/airspace system contribution explicit: capacity, safety, operations, demand, routing, scheduling, uncertainty, and integration.

Useful sources:
- IEEE T-ITS scope: https://ieee-itss.org/pub/t-its/
- T-ITS paper guidelines: https://ieee-itss.org/wp-content/uploads/T-ITS-Paper_Submission_Guidelines.pdf

## IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials, IEEE TWC, IEEE IoT-J, and Communications Magazine

Editorial signal:
- IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials is a premier venue for surveys and tutorials in communications and networking; both article types should be tutorial in nature and understandable outside the specialty.
- COMST survey articles should cover development from inception to current state and beyond, show interactions with other areas, and make the article category and scope explicit in the abstract and introduction.
- COMST expects clear differentiation from prior surveys/tutorials, up-to-date bibliography, strong taxonomies, comparative tables, and synthesis.
- IEEE Communications Magazine manuscripts should be tutorial in nature and comprehensible to the average communications reader; special issues and feature topics require narrow, timely scope.
- IEEE TWC requires the contribution to be centered on a wireless communications problem; wireless channel, fading, interference, power, bandwidth, or resource allocation should matter.
- IEEE IoT-J publishes advances and review articles on IoT architecture, enabling technologies, communication and networking protocols, services, applications, standards, and testbeds.
- IoT-J special issues show active interest in UAV networks, space-air-ground integrated IoT, trajectory design, resource allocation, MEC, spectrum management, and security.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For air-ground cooperative networking, distinguish whether the core is wireless theory, IoT systems, mobile computing, or transportation operations.
- Do not present UAVs merely as a use case; explain how aerial mobility changes networking assumptions and design tradeoffs.
- If targeting COMST, explicitly state whether the article is a survey or tutorial, the covered time period/scope, how it differs from prior surveys, and what taxonomy or framework it contributes.

## ACM Computing Surveys

Editorial signal:
- ACM Computing Surveys publishes surveys and tutorials that integrate existing computing literature and put results in context.
- A survey emphasizes classification, perspective, and trends; a tutorial emphasizes basic concepts and concrete examples for inexpert readers.
- Acceptability depends on high technical quality, relevance, general interest, and effective presentation.

Review-manuscript implication:
- For spatiotemporal intelligence, foundation models, graph learning, edge intelligence, or distributed systems aspects, a CSUR-style review must contribute an organizing framework and explain what the literature means to researchers and practitioners.
- Do not frame new technical results as the main contribution.

Useful sources:
- ACM CSUR journal page: https://dl.acm.org/journal/csur
- CSUR editorial charter: http://codes-isss.org/surveys_subdomain/charter/
- CSUR author information: https://www.codes-isss.org/csur_subdomain/author_info/

## Ethics, Confidentiality, and AI Use

Use these as general publication-process constraints:
- Peer review should be objective, constructive, and confidential.
- AI use may need disclosure depending on the target journal.
- Authors remain responsible for factual accuracy, originality, citations, and permissions.

Useful sources:
- COPE peer reviewer guidelines: https://publicationethics.org/guidance/guideline/ethical-guidelines-peer-reviewers
- ICMJE AI use: https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/ai-use-by-authors.html
- Nature Portfolio AI policy pages are linked from individual journal editorial policies.
