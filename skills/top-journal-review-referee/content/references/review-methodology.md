# Review Methodology

Use this reference to choose an appropriate review standard without forcing the wrong template.

## Match Method to Article Type

### Narrative Review

Best for authoritative synthesis, concept building, field framing, and future agenda.

Expect:
- Clear importance and timeliness.
- Concrete aims or guiding questions.
- Transparent but not necessarily exhaustive source selection.
- Balanced interpretation of evidence.
- Explicit limitations and omissions.
- Strong figures/tables that synthesize.

Use a SANRA-inspired check for narrative quality:
1. Is the importance justified for the target readership?
2. Are aims or questions concrete?
3. Is the literature search or source-selection logic described enough to judge coverage?
4. Are key statements referenced appropriately?
5. Is scientific/technical reasoning based on appropriate evidence?
6. Are central data, metrics, or examples presented accurately and comparably?

Do not call this "SANRA scoring" unless the user explicitly wants formal SANRA. Use it as a reasoning checklist.

### Perspective

Best for an original, forward-looking viewpoint on a fast-moving or emerging topic.

Expect:
- A narrower but sharper thesis.
- Balanced treatment of alternatives even when opinionated.
- A future-facing framework or research agenda.
- Fewer exhaustive details; stronger argumentation.

### Roadmap

Best for outlining challenges, milestones, dependencies, and research/deployment pathways.

Expect:
- Time horizons or maturity levels.
- Technical, regulatory, data, operational, and infrastructure dependencies.
- Testable milestones and bottlenecks.
- Clear distinction between near-term engineering and long-term scientific challenges.

### Technical Review or Survey

Best for methods, systems, benchmarks, algorithms, and engineering practices.

Expect:
- Taxonomy that improves understanding.
- Comparative tables with compatible metrics and assumptions.
- Dataset, benchmark, and reproducibility analysis.
- Discussion of limitations, failure modes, and deployment constraints.

### Systematic or Scoping Review

Use only when the manuscript claims systematic/scoping coverage or the target journal requires it.

Expect PRISMA/PRISMA-ScR-style transparency:
- Databases and sources searched.
- Search strings or search logic.
- Date range and update date.
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria.
- Screening process.
- Data extraction/coding scheme.
- Synthesis method.
- Limitations and risk of bias or evidence-quality assessment where appropriate.

Do not require meta-analysis unless the review question demands quantitative synthesis and the field evidence supports it.

## Evidence and Citation Audit

Check every major claim type:

| Claim type | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| "Rapidly growing field" | bibliometric trend, special issues, standards, deployments, funding/programs, or clear literature growth |
| "State of the art" | latest representative high-quality papers, benchmark leaders, or authoritative surveys |
| "Outperforms" | comparable datasets, metrics, baselines, and statistical/uncertainty treatment |
| "Operationally feasible" | field tests, prototypes, regulatory compatibility, safety and reliability evidence |
| "Scalable" | complexity analysis, large-area/multi-agent experiments, simulation at realistic scale, or deployment evidence |
| "Robust" | cross-domain tests, sensitivity analysis, stress tests, uncertainty estimates, failure cases |
| "Integrated system" | explicit coupling among sensing, planning, communications, computation, and operations |

Flag unsupported claims using:
- **Needs source**: no citation or evidence.
- **Overclaim**: evidence supports a weaker statement.
- **Mismatched source**: citation is about a different scale, platform, or problem.
- **Stale source**: citation is outdated for a fast-moving area.
- **Missing counterevidence**: major conflicting results or limitations absent.

## Reference-List Quality

Check:
- Recent primary work from the last 2-5 years for fast-moving topics.
- Foundational work where needed for context.
- Balance across regions, research communities, and methods.
- Limited self-citation unless justified by the field.
- Primary sources over secondary citations for key technical claims.
- No citation padding.
- No missing seminal datasets, benchmarks, standards, or survey papers.

## Search Transparency for Narrative Reviews

A high-level narrative review can include a concise "review approach" paragraph without becoming systematic:

- Databases or sources used.
- Keywords/topic families.
- Date range.
- Criteria for prioritizing literature.
- Types of work included: peer-reviewed papers, standards, reports, datasets, testbeds.
- Known exclusions and why.

This protects against reviewer concerns about cherry-picking.

## Figures, Tables, and Boxes

Ask whether each display item does one of these:
- Compresses a taxonomy.
- Explains a system architecture.
- Compares evidence under compatible assumptions.
- Reveals tradeoffs.
- Shows maturity or readiness.
- Converts a long paragraph into a decision aid.

Delete or redesign display items that merely decorate or repeat text.

## AI Use and Integrity

When the manuscript used AI assistance:
- Advise checking the target journal's AI disclosure policy.
- Never list AI as an author.
- Ensure all AI-assisted text is fact-checked.
- Verify all references exist and support the relevant claims.
- Check permissions for reused figures and adapted diagrams.

## Revision Strategy

Prioritize changes in this order:

1. Target journal fit and article type.
2. Central thesis and scope.
3. Structure and section logic.
4. Evidence coverage and citation integrity.
5. Figures/tables and synthesis devices.
6. English polish.
7. Formatting and house style.

If the manuscript has structural problems, do not spend most of the response on grammar.
